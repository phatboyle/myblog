Florida Divide last few days
Fishing for Alligators, netting tilapia, bobcat, Okeechobee, Miami
~500 miles total
The trail campsites I had to pick from had names like “Rattlesnake Hammock” and “Tick Island Slough.”  I opted for a private fancy campground called Riverranch.  Even had a pizza and ice cream place.
Got up in the morning and headed south  to some locks where divers were inspecting the infrastructure.   I asked them if they worried about gators.  They pointed to the other side of the locks, where fish and game rangers were clearing gators out for the divers by casting  thick fishing line over them.  When the gator feels the line, it rolls sideways as a reaction and winds itself up.  Then they reel it in and put it in a big pickup for relocation.  I tried to get close to watch but the supervisor would not allow.  I didn’t ask now they new if they got all of them.
Passed a guy fishing for tilapia by casting a net into a waterway.  I watched as he threw the net 3 times from shore and managed to fill a bucket with around 8 big Talapia.  These are invasive fish in Florida, originally from Africa.
Saw my first bobcat (no pic) in the  notorious Kissimmee prairie state park that was deliberately burned by the state park rangers a week before (controlled burn to manage vegetation).  Only had to wade a few knee deep puddles.
Met a few hikers going northbound on the Florida trail:
Matt from California is in between jobs and hiking from the south end of Florida all the way to Pensacola!  He told stories of wading in chest deep water in Big Cyprus reserve in south Florida.  No thank you.  Previous hikes included the Colorado trail and Oregon coastal trail.
Bob from Florida is section hiking the Florida trail. He’s done many long distance adventures, including his bike across the US.
Stopped trip in Okeechobee to get back to Seattle for our annual backcountry ski race at Meany lodge.  Okeechobee is a cattle farming town on the north end of the second largest lake in the US (Lake Michigan is #1, Lake Superior doesn’t count because it’s split with Canada). Florida ranchers have 900,000 head of cattle and Okeechobee is the center of the cattle economy with many farm equipment and feed stores.
Was hoping to catch an Amtrak train to Miami but they wouldn’t take the bike at the Okeechobee stop for unclear reasons (they claimed they couldn’t take baggage at Oke, though this wasn’t noted on the website).  Maybe because the train is blocking traffic in the main drag through town while parked at the platform.  Got to Miami by car and toured around on Thursday checking out some of the neighborhoods like South Beach, Coral Gables and cool graffiti murals of Wynwood.
Thanks to Karlos @singletracksamurai for such a cool route - I’ll be back to do the panhandle section.
