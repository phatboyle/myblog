Leshi, Madrona and Mt Baker, Seattle Parks.
Rode a loop down Lake Washington Boulevard on a rainy Thursday, climbing up through Leshi then down to I90 then back north.
The steep hills, ravines, ridgetops, shoreline and infrastructure in this area really is fun to explore.
Route Notes:
35th and E James has one of the best viewpoints in Seattle (just south on 35th)
It’s a steep walk up from beloved Madrona Park on Lake Washington through the Madrona Ravine to Alvin Larkins Park (big trees!) to Madrona Playground, which has a cool “shelter place.”
Peppi’s playground, adjacent to Leshi Elementary is named by students after their classmate that passed away in 71. Worthwhile.
Drop down on curvy Lake Dell Ave through Leshi Lake Dell Natural area to Day Street boat ramp underneath I90 bridge.
Steep long climb up many stairs to Mt Baker Ridge Viewpoint - a great view west and a cool sunset dial that tells the season by slots in rocks that are angled to point at the setting sun.  This is a delightful little installation and a worthwhile visit at sunset to see its design and visit nearby coffee shops.
West on I90 bike trail to Sam Smith Park, Blue Dog Pond (dog park, and what looks like a stormwater overflow), Coleman Park, Benvenuto Viewpoint (a wide spot in the trail) and Atlantic Street Park.
Jimi Hendrix Park(!!) and the adjacent Northwest African American Museum.
On the north side of I90:
Judge Charles Stokes Overlook, honoring the first black judge on the King County District Court.
Judkins Park is expansive and has neat terraces with different fields, paths, courts and a skatepark at each level. It even has a new Link station for the I90 route.  I was impressed - this park is designed to be used.
Rode north through Dr Blanche Levizzo Park which is a really neat narrow park between buildings with nice features, and small amphitheaters.  Dr Levizzo was the first African American woman pediatrition in Washington State.
Spruce Street Mini park
Miller Pickleball Courts which were wet and covered with leaves, but are lit and a good spot to play at night when it’s dry.
Today riding home, I took 21st through Capitol Hill, rather than my usual route on 19th.    In the span of 1.5 miles, I saw Meany Middle School, Washington Middle School,  Holy Names Academy, and the cool corner of 21st and Union, with the Central Cinema, CommuniTea Kombucha and other establishments keeping things real in Seattle like Heart to Heart (conversation community) and  Chuck’s Hop Shop.
