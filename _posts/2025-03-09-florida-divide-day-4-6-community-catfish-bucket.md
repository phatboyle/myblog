Florida divide day 4-6.  Community catfish bucket, Orlando wetland park
Day 4: Palatka to Alexander Springs 85m
Crossed the locks at the Rodman reservoir, a remnant from a failed attempt to make a Panama Canal across Florida. Now it’s a popular recreation area and refuge for manatees looking for warm water.
Talked with a guy catching a catfish that he  donated to the “community bucket” which one of the locals picks up.  Then he went back to fishing for crappie or bass, which he does keep to eat.
Then a long ride through the Oxala national forest.
Camped at Alexander Springs, a swimming hole supplied by 72 degree water from the underground Florida aquifer.
Day 5: Alexander Springs to Oviedo area 60m
Early ride through the Paisley Forest.  This is where the first segment of the Florida National Trail was made in 1968.  Saw Florida deer with big white bushy tail. Then through some refuges and around the outskirts of Orlando and a nice ride on the Cross Seminole county bike trail.
Got to the campsite at Lake mills to find it locked at 9:00 pm.  So camped across the street by the cow pasture.
Day 6: Oviedo to Melbourne 65m
Early morning with some feral pigs on route to the Orlando Wetland Park (city operated refuge), covered with birds and alligators.  Highly recommended.  It has 1/2 mile boardwalk over the lake with good views of the spoonbill and wood stork rookeries.
Ran into 3 Florida trail hikers while we were wading through a small swamp. They assured me that alligators and snakes will all spook and run away. Maybe, but I think these  Florida hikers are more hard core than me, with all the heat and creepy crawlies they deal with.  While we were taking, a big pig came out of the forest, contemplated us and then kept going.
There are some sections on this route on faster paved roads to connect to the next section.   It’s kind of depressing to see all the roadkill on them.  Many animals just don’t have a chance dealing with fast traffic and no way to easily cross.  I wish there were more that could be done to improve the roads.  Maybe with more culverts and underpasses.
